Homes in Rose Valley & Wallingford

We build for the house you actually have.

Rose Valley began in 1901 as an Arts & Crafts colony founded by architect William Lightfoot Price, who renovated the old mill houses and designed new homes. Nearly the whole borough is now a National Register historic district.

Wallingford, settled in 1687 and grown up around its Furness-attributed train station, sits right next door. Here, hand-built detail is the point. We repair and replicate it rather than replace it with something generic.

Permits & approvals
We handle the paperwork.

Every project is permitted, inspected and closed out properly. Here's what applies in Rose Valley & Wallingford:

Rose Valley Borough Office · Nether Providence Township (Wallingford)
Official permit info →
  • Rose Valley: permits go through the Borough Office at Old Mill Lane. We confirm current forms and any historic review before design.
  • Nether Providence: structural changes (adding or removing walls), additions and demolition need permits, with two sets of plans.
  • The Township's Historical Commission comments on demolition and on work within 300 feet of historic sites.
Questions from Rose Valley & Wallingford homeowners
Good to know.
Can you work on a historic Rose Valley house?

Yes. Respecting original materials is how we work. We repair what can be saved and replicate what can't, matching the original profiles.

What permits does Nether Providence require for an addition?

Additions, structural wall changes and demolition all need permits, with two sets of plans. We prepare and file them.
