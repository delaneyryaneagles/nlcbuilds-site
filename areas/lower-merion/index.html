Homes in Lower Merion

We build for the house you actually have.

Lower Merion's great houses were built from the 1870s to the 1930s, many for Pennsylvania Railroad and Baldwin Locomotive executives. Historians have documented 158 country houses from that era, and the township was marketed for its stone construction and tree-lined streets.

Stone walls, plaster, old-growth trim and a century of past repairs: these homes reward patience. We take the time to match materials, keep the character, and leave the house better than we found it.

Projects that fit Lower Merion homes.

Windows, doors & porches →

Lower Merion has seven local historic districts, including Gladwyne/Merion Square, Haverford Station and Ardmore. Visible exterior changes there go through HARB, and we prepare the submission.

Decks sized smart →

Under Lower Merion's impervious rules, a deck of 200 SF or less doesn't count against your lot. Over 200 SF, half the area counts. We design to that line.

Additions →

Additions need an impervious-surface calculation, and more than 1,500 SF of disturbance can require an engineered plan plus an erosion-control permit. We handle it.

Questions from Lower Merion homeowners
Good to know.
Is my Lower Merion home in a historic district?

Seven local districts are reviewed by HARB, including Gladwyne/Merion Square and Haverford Station. We check your address before design so exterior plans pass review.

How big can my deck be before it counts as impervious?

In Lower Merion, decks of 200 SF or less don't count. Above that, half the deck area counts, assuming a porous surface underneath. We'll size yours to make the most of it.
