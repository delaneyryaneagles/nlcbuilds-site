Homes in Wayne & Radnor

We build for the house you actually have.

Wayne was one of America's first planned suburbs. In the 1880s Drexel & Childs bought about 600 acres and built the Wayne Estate, and much of North Wayne went up between 1881 and 1935, with Queen Anne detail still on its porches and gables.

These houses deserve a builder who matches what's there rather than covering it up. That's how we work: true divided-lite windows where they belong, porch columns and rails milled to the original profiles, and kitchens and baths that feel modern without fighting the house.

Permits & approvals
We handle the paperwork.

Every project is permitted, inspected and closed out properly. Here's what applies in Wayne & Radnor:

Radnor Township Community Development
Official permit info →
  • Most construction needs a Radnor permit, and every contractor must be registered with the Township. We handle both.
  • Historic districts (North Wayne, South Wayne, Louella Court): HARB meets the first Wednesday of the month. We submit drawings and photos and attend for you.
  • Stormwater: a grading permit is needed for roughly 500–999 SF of new impervious surface, and a full plan at 1,000 SF or more. It must be approved before a building permit is accepted.
Questions from Wayne & Radnor homeowners
Good to know.
Do I need HARB approval for my project in Wayne?

If your home is in the North Wayne, South Wayne or Louella Court historic districts, new construction and additions go through Radnor's Historical Architectural Review Board. We'll check your address and tell you up front.

Will a new deck or addition trigger stormwater rules in Radnor?

It can. Radnor counts new and replaced hard surface: around 500 SF needs a grading permit and 1,000 SF needs a full stormwater management plan. We design to the rules before you spend a dollar.
